A SMALL PROJECT WITH BRIGHT IDEAS

Climbing holds.
Unexpected behavior.

Rave Board started with a simple thought at a climbing gym in Durango, Colorado: all those colorful lights could probably do something pretty fun.

So now they do. Spirals, lasers, falling rain, local logos, and messages drifting across the wall. Something for gym events, celebrations, and the few minutes between climbs.

Made by Braden. Kept small on purpose. Open the site, pick a light show, and enjoy it.

AT THE WALL

From preview to party.

  1. 01

    Choose an effect.

    Open the Rave page and try the on-screen preview. The speed slider takes every effect from slow melt to full rave.

  2. 02

    Match your board.

    Choose the same layout, size, and hold sets as in the Kilter app. The default is Original 12×12 with kickboard. Changing the wall angle doesn’t change the light map.

  3. 03

    Connect and check.

    With the gym’s okay and nobody on the wall, disconnect the Kilter app or any other phone controlling the board. Click Connect board, then Test corners to check that the lights match the layout.

  4. 04

    Play. Then clear.

    Press Play on board and keep the page visible. Stop & clear sends a blackout. Disconnect now releases Bluetooth if it gets stuck, but can leave the last picture lit. The Kilter app can replace that picture with a climb.

A FEW USEFUL THINGS

Before the lights go on.

What do I need?

A powered Kilter Board, permission to use it, and a device with Bluetooth. Chrome or Edge on a Windows or Mac computer is the starting point. The Rave page checks your browser before offering a connection.

Can I use my phone?

The preview works on phones. Mobile board connections are still being tested. iPhone and iPad are preview-only in this release; the Rave page will tell you what is available on your device.

Do I need an account or extra hardware?

No account is needed. Rave Board sends light commands through the board’s existing Bluetooth controller. It doesn’t modify the board’s firmware.

Why does the wall look different from the preview?

Climbing holds make a wonderfully odd screen. They’re spaced far apart, the LEDs have a limited color palette, and Bluetooth speed varies. Simple shapes and large letters usually read best. If fast motion looks jumpy, turn the speed down.

What if it won’t connect?

Turn on Bluetooth, move close to the board, and disconnect any other device controlling it. Check the browser message on the Rave page. Connection settings include a slower sending option if the lights are glitchy.

Is this an official Kilter app?

No. This is an independent project, not affiliated with Kilter or Aurora Climbing. It is an early release; controller and browser behavior can vary.

BUILT ON GOOD WORK

The Bluetooth protocol and board maps build on Grip Connect and the community’s Kilter protocol research. Thank you to the people who figured this out and shared it.
