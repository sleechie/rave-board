About Rave Board

Made by Braden for the Kilter Board at Gravity Lab in Durango, Colorado. The idea was to use the board's lights for something besides marking climbs.

It plays animated patterns and scrolls messages across the holds, including a Gravity Lab lettering sequence. Everything runs in the browser and uses the board's existing Bluetooth controller.

How to use it

  1. Preview an effect

    Open Rave and choose a pattern. Adjust the speed and brightness before connecting.

  2. Match your board

    Select the same layout, size, and hold sets as in the Kilter app. The default is Original 12 × 12 with kickboard. The wall angle does not affect the light map.

  3. Connect and check

    Ask the gym staff and wait until nobody is on the wall. Disconnect other devices controlling the board, then select Connect board. Use Test corners to check the layout.

  4. Play, then clear the lights

    Select Play on board and keep the page open. When finished, use Stop & clear. If the connection stalls, Disconnect now releases Bluetooth but may leave the last picture lit. Reconnect with the Kilter app to replace it.

Questions

Which devices work?

Start with Chrome or Edge on a Windows or Mac computer with Bluetooth. Mobile connections are still being tested. iPhone and iPad are preview-only in this release. The Rave page checks your browser before offering a connection.

Do I need an account or extra hardware?

No. The website connects to the board's existing controller and does not change its firmware.

Why does the wall look different from the preview?

The Original board preview uses traced hold shapes with approximate glow. The LEDs have a limited color palette, and Bluetooth speed varies. Simple shapes and large letters read best. Turn the speed down if movement looks jumpy.

What if it won't connect?

Turn on Bluetooth, move closer, and disconnect any other device controlling the board. Check the browser notice on the Rave page. If it connects but the lights are glitchy, try a slower option in Connection settings.

Credits & privacy

The Bluetooth protocol and hold maps build on Grip Connect and the community's Kilter protocol research. Open-source licence.

The hold-shape viewer uses traced board artwork from Boardsesh. Shape sources and attribution and Apache licence.

Rave Board is independent of Kilter, Aurora Climbing, and Gravity Lab.

Your board settings are saved in your browser. Light commands go directly from your device to the board.
